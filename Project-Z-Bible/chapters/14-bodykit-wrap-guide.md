# Body Kit & Wrap Guide

> **📌 Note:** Cosmetics are explicitly optional in this book's plan — see the
> [Budget Tracker](#budget-tracker) contingency note. Nothing in this chapter should compete
> with Phase 1 or Phase 2 performance budget unless there's genuine headroom.

## Design intent

The Lavender & Charcoal theme of this handbook can extend to the car itself if you choose
to wrap or repaint: a clean, technical, blueprint-style look rather than a loud graphics
package — matte or satin charcoal as the primary body tone with lavender used sparingly as
an accent (stripes, brake calipers, badges) reads as premium rather than "wrapped car."

## Body kit considerations

| Component | Function | Consideration for this build |
|---|---|---|
| Front bumper/lip | Front aero, cosmetic refresh | Confirm clearance for the intercooler piping added in [Phase 2](#phase-2-the-500-hp-plan) before ordering |
| Side skirts | Cosmetic, minor aero | Low-hanging risk on driveways/speed bumps at lowered ride height — see [Suspension Guide](#suspension-guide) |
| Rear diffuser/spoiler | Cosmetic, minor aero at speed | Functional aero benefit is minimal below track speeds; treat as styling |
| Hood (vented) | Cosmetic + can aid underhood heat extraction | Genuinely useful once turbocharged — underhood temps rise with boost |

> **⚠️ Caution:** Any front-end body kit ordered before the turbo kit is finalized risks a
> fitment conflict with the intercooler piping. Finalize [Turbo Planning Guide](#turbo-planning-guide)
> hardware choices before committing to front bumper/lip purchases.

## Wrap vs. paint

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Vinyl wrap | Reversible, wide color/finish range, protects factory paint underneath | Needs periodic replacement (typically 5–7 years), edges can lift if poorly installed |
| Paint correction + ceramic coating (factory paint) | Preserves originality, often best long-term value if paint is in good shape | Doesn't allow a color change; paint correction can't fix deep damage |
| Full repaint | Permanent color change, can fix real paint issues | Most expensive option, not reversible |

> **💡 Tip:** For a car this book assumes you'll drive hard and eventually sell or trade,
> a quality wrap over solid factory paint is usually the best cost/flexibility trade-off —
> it protects the original paint and can be removed without affecting resale.

## Theme application checklist

> **✅ Checklist — if applying the Lavender & Charcoal theme to the car**
> - [ ] Primary body: matte or satin charcoal
> - [ ] Accent: lavender used sparingly — stripe, brake calipers, or badge accents only
> - [ ] Wheels: charcoal or gunmetal finish to stay in the theme's palette
> - [ ] Avoid mixing in unrelated accent colors — keep the palette to the two tones plus fog
>   (off-white) for any lettering/pinstripe detail, matching `templates/theme.yaml`

## Install/quality checklist for wraps

> **✅ Checklist**
> - [ ] Vehicle fully washed and clayed before wrap install — contamination under vinyl
>   causes bubbling
> - [ ] Panel edges wrapped/tucked, not just cut and left exposed (edges lift first)
> - [ ] Any pre-existing paint chips or scratches noted/photographed before wrap (for
>   warranty/insurance clarity)
> - [ ] Installer provides care instructions (wash method, wax/coating compatibility)
> - [ ] Confirm any panels removed for the [Turbo Planning Guide](#turbo-planning-guide)
>   install (bumper, hood) are wrapped/finished before final reassembly, not after
