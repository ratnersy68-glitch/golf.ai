# Inspection Checklist

> **📌 Note:** Print this chapter and take it to the pre-purchase inspection, or use it as
> your own PPI (pre-purchase inspection) sheet if you're doing it yourself. Pair with the
> known weak points in the [Buyer's Guide](#the-350z-buyers-guide).

## Before you go: what to bring

> **✅ Checklist**
> - [ ] This chapter, printed, or on your phone
> - [ ] A flashlight (for underneath the car and inside wheel wells)
> - [ ] An OBD-II scanner, or a mechanic/PPI service that provides one
> - [ ] A magnet (a cheap, non-scratching one) — helps spot filler/bodywork on steel panels,
>   since filler is non-magnetic and steel is
> - [ ] A friend, if possible — a second set of eyes and ears during the test drive
> - [ ] Payment for a professional PPI if you're not doing this inspection yourself

## How long this actually takes

| Approach | Time needed |
|---|---|
| Full self-inspection using this chapter | 60–90 minutes, unrushed |
| Professional PPI at a shop | 1–2 hours, usually while you wait or same-day |
| Quick "is it even worth a full look" first pass | 15–20 minutes (walk-around + cold start sections only) |

> **⚠️ Caution:** If a seller won't give you 60–90 minutes, won't allow a test drive, or
> won't allow a professional PPI at a shop of your choosing, treat that as a red flag on its
> own — see [Buyer's Guide](#the-350z-buyers-guide) red flags.

## Before you even start the engine

> **✅ Checklist — walk-around**
> - [ ] Panel gaps even on all four corners (hood, doors, hatch)
> - [ ] Paint depth/thickness consistent across panels (paint meter if available) — flags
>   prior bodywork
> - [ ] Tire brand/wear matches across axle; uneven wear = alignment or suspension issue
> - [ ] Tire date codes (DOT code) — anything over 6 years old should be budgeted for
>   replacement regardless of tread
> - [ ] Frame rails and subframe — no rust-through, no signs of prior impact
> - [ ] Exhaust hangers intact, no dragging or excessive rust-through on the exhaust system
> - [ ] Under-hood: no fresh silicone/RTV in unusual places (sign of a hidden leak repair)
> - [ ] Fluid level and color: engine oil, coolant, transmission, brake, power steering
> - [ ] VIN plate matches title/registration; door jamb sticker present and legible

### For beginners: how to actually check these

> **💡 Tips — what "good" looks like when you've never checked before**
> - **Panel gaps:** stand back and sight down each gap (door-to-fender, hood-to-fender) —
>   they should look like a consistent, even ribbon of space all the way down. A gap that's
>   wider at one end, or different side-to-side, suggests a panel was removed/replaced.
> - **Paint depth meter:** a small handheld gadget that reads factory paint thickness in
>   microns; most panels on an unrepaired car read similarly. A panel reading much thicker
>   suggests body filler or extra paint layers from repair work. Not essential, but cheap to
>   borrow/rent and removes guesswork.
> - **Tire DOT code:** a 4-digit code stamped on the tire sidewall, last 4 digits of the DOT
>   number — first two digits are the week, last two are the year (e.g., "2319" = 23rd week
>   of 2019). Tires this old should be budgeted for replacement even with tread left, since
>   rubber hardens and cracks with age regardless of use.
> - **The magnet test:** gently run a small magnet over painted steel panels (fenders, doors,
>   quarters) — it should stick firmly everywhere. A spot where it doesn't stick, or sticks
>   weakly, suggests body filler underneath.

## Cold start

> **✅ Checklist**
> - [ ] Engine starts within 1–2 seconds of cranking, no extended cranking
> - [ ] Listen for 1–2 seconds of timing chain rattle max — anything longer/louder flags
>   guides/tensioner wear
> - [ ] No blue smoke (oil burning) or black smoke (over-fueling) from exhaust on startup
> - [ ] All dash warning lights illuminate then clear (bulb check), none remain lit
> - [ ] Idle stabilizes within ~30–60 seconds, no excessive hunting

> **🛑 Critical:** Insist the engine is stone cold when you arrive — ideally not started that
> day. A seller who "just wants to warm it up before you get there" is often hiding a
> cold-start symptom (rattle, rough idle, smoke) that disappears once the engine's warm.

## Engine bay, running

> **✅ Checklist**
> - [ ] No visible oil leaks at valve cover, oil pan, or timing chain cover
> - [ ] No coolant leaks at radiator, hoses, water pump, or reservoir
> - [ ] Belts free of cracking/glazing, no squeal on startup
> - [ ] Idle is smooth — no misfire feel, no rough idle
> - [ ] No unusual knocking, ticking, or rod-knock-like sounds under light throttle blips
> - [ ] AC blows cold, all electrical accessories function (windows, locks, lights, horn)

## Test drive

> **✅ Checklist**
> - [ ] Automatic transmission shifts smoothly through all gears, cold and after 10+ minutes
>   warm
> - [ ] No flare (RPM rise before gear catches) on upshifts, no delayed engagement in
>   Drive/Reverse
> - [ ] No clunk from the differential on deceleration or throttle on/off transitions
> - [ ] No whine that changes pitch with road speed (differential/wheel bearing)
> - [ ] Steering tracks straight, no pulling, no vague/wandering feel
> - [ ] No clunks over bumps (control arm bushings, sway bar end links)
> - [ ] Brakes stop straight, no pulsation (warped rotor) or grinding
> - [ ] No warning lights appear during the drive (VDC, ABS, SLIP, check engine)

### Test drive route checklist

> **💡 Tip:** A parking-lot loop tells you almost nothing. Ask for a route that includes all
> of the following, in this order:
> - [ ] A slow-speed section with full-lock turns both directions (listen for CV joint/steering clunks)
> - [ ] A stretch long enough to reach normal operating temperature (10+ minutes)
> - [ ] A highway on-ramp or similar for a full-throttle pull through several gears
> - [ ] A decelerate-from-speed section, off-throttle, listening for differential clunk/whine
> - [ ] Several stops from moderate speed, checking for straight, pulsation-free braking
> - [ ] A rough or bumpy road section, listening for suspension clunks/rattles

## Post-drive checks

> **✅ Checklist**
> - [ ] Recheck all fluid levels for any drop after the drive
> - [ ] Look underneath for fresh drips after the car has been running/driven
> - [ ] Check transmission fluid smell/color if accessible — burnt smell or dark brown color
>   is a red flag
> - [ ] Re-scan for codes — some issues only set codes after a full drive cycle

## Diagnostic scan

> **✅ Checklist**
> - [ ] Full OBD-II scan for stored and pending codes (not just the ones triggering the
>   dash light)
> - [ ] Check readiness monitors — all/most "ready" suggests no recent battery
>   disconnect/code-clearing to hide an issue
> - [ ] Pull freeze-frame data on any stored codes, especially misfire (P0300–P0306) or
>   knock-related codes
> - [ ] Check transmission-specific codes if scan tool supports manufacturer-specific PIDs

> **📌 Note for beginners:** "Readiness monitors" are internal self-tests the car's computer
> runs continuously (for emissions systems, oxygen sensors, catalytic converters, etc). When
> a battery is disconnected or codes are cleared, these reset to "not ready" and take time
> (and specific drive conditions) to complete again. Mostly "not ready" monitors on a car
> that's supposedly been driven normally is a hint that something was recently reset —
> possibly to hide a code.

## Scoring sheet

| Category | Pass | Minor issue (negotiate) | Major issue (walk or big discount) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body/frame | No rust, even gaps | Minor surface rust, panel gap | Frame rust-through, poor collision repair |
| Engine | Smooth idle, no leaks, brief cold rattle | Minor oil seep, longer cold rattle | Persistent rattle, smoke, misfire |
| Transmission | Smooth shifts, no flare | Occasional harsh shift when cold | Flares, slips, clunks into gear |
| Suspension/Steering | Tight, no clunks | Minor bushing wear | Vague steering, worn-out shocks |
| Brakes | Straight stop, no pulsation | Minor pad wear | Pulsation, grinding, warning light |
| Electrical | Everything works | One minor accessory issue | Multiple failures, unexplained warning lights |

> **🛑 Critical:** Any single item in the "Major issue" column should reset your offer or end
> the negotiation — these are the ones that turn a $22,000 budget build into a $30,000 one.

## Simple point-scoring version (optional, for comparing multiple cars)

If you're looking at several candidate cars, a simple point system makes comparison easier
than relying on memory and gut feeling:

| Category | Pass = 2 pts | Minor issue = 1 pt | Major issue = 0 pts (and stop scoring — it's a walk-away) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body/frame | | | |
| Engine | | | |
| Transmission | | | |
| Suspension/Steering | | | |
| Brakes | | | |
| Electrical | | | |

Total possible: 12. Anything scoring a 0 in any single category should be treated as a
walk-away regardless of the total score — a great total with one major issue hidden inside
it is still a car with a major issue.

## What if the seller won't allow a full inspection?

> **⚠️ Caution — common pushback, and how to respond**

| Seller says... | Reasonable response |
|---|---|
| "It's already sold as-is, no inspection needed" | As-is doesn't mean uninspectable — you can still look, you just can't demand repairs after |
| "I don't have time for all that today" | Offer to schedule a return visit or a shop PPI — a legitimate seller can accommodate this |
| "Nobody else has asked for this much inspection" | That's not a reason to skip it — it's a reason other buyers may have overpaid |
| Refuses a professional PPI at a shop of your choice entirely | Serious red flag — walk away |

## Frequently asked questions

> **Q: I don't have an OBD-II scanner. Can I still do a meaningful inspection?**
> Yes — most auto parts stores will scan codes for free, and many will do it in a parking
> lot with advance notice. A cheap Bluetooth OBD-II adapter paired with a free phone app is
> also a worthwhile ~$20 investment if you'll be looking at multiple cars.

> **Q: What if the car passes everything but I still have a bad feeling?**
> Trust it enough to sleep on the decision, but try to articulate *why* — is it a specific
> checklist item that felt borderline, or genuinely just nerves about a big purchase? If you
> can point to something concrete, revisit that item. If it's genuinely just nerves, that's
> normal for a first project car and not itself a reason to walk.

> **Q: Is it rude to bring a checklist this detailed to look at someone's car?**
> No — a good-faith seller expects and often appreciates a serious buyer. This is a
> meaningful purchase for both sides; treating it that way is normal, not rude.
