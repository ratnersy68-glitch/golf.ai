# Maintenance Log

> **📌 Note:** This log tracks routine maintenance — fluids, wear items, inspections. Track
> performance modifications separately in the [Modification Log](#modification-log). A
> reusable blank entry template is available at
> `templates/maintenance-log-entry-template.md`.

## Why keep a log at all (for first-timers)

It's tempting to think you'll remember what was done and when — most owners don't, six
months later, and definitely not three years later. A log does three concrete things a
memory can't:

1. **Proves service history** to a future buyer or your own future self, raising resale
   value and trust.
2. **Reveals patterns** — if the same symptom keeps recurring, a log shows that instantly;
   memory alone often doesn't connect the dots.
3. **Protects warranty/insurance claims** — dated, detailed records are exactly what's asked
   for if a claim or warranty dispute ever comes up.

## Paper vs. digital — pick what you'll actually maintain

| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| This printed chapter, filled in by hand | Always with the car if kept in the glovebox, no app/login needed | Limited space, harder to search later |
| A spreadsheet (copy this chapter's table structure) | Easy to search/sort, easy to back up | Requires remembering to update it away from the garage |
| A notes app on your phone | Always in your pocket, quick to update in the moment | Less structured unless you keep a consistent format |

> **💡 Tip:** The best system is the one you'll actually use in the moment, standing in the
> garage with dirty hands. A perfect system you never update is worse than an imperfect one
> you do.

## What to write down when something goes wrong (not just routine service)

> **💡 Tip:** For an unexpected symptom (not a planned service), capture more than the log
> table's columns allow — use the Notes column generously, or add a line like this:
>
> ```
> Date: ____________  Odometer: ____________
> Symptom: (what you heard/felt/saw, and under what conditions)
> When it started: (sudden, or gradually worsening?)
> Conditions: (cold/warm engine, speed, load, weather)
> What you did about it: (nothing yet / scanned codes / took to shop)
> Outcome: (resolved how, or still open)
> ```

## Frequently asked questions

> **Q: Do I need to log things the dealer/shop already has on record?**
> Yes — don't rely on a shop's own records surviving indefinitely or being easy to retrieve
> later. Your own log is the copy you control.

> **Q: What if I bought the car used and don't have earlier history?**
> Note that clearly at the top of your log ("prior history unknown/incomplete as of purchase,
> odometer X") so it's clear to future-you (or a future buyer) where your records start.

> **Q: Should I log DIY work the same as shop work?**
> Yes, identically — the point is a complete record of what's been done to the car,
> regardless of who did it.
